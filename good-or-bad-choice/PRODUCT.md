# Product

## Register

product

## Users

One person, several times a day, deciding whether the thing they just did was a
good call or a bad one. They are not journalling and they are not being coached.
They want the smallest possible gesture — one tap, no typing, no category, no
note — and then, sometimes, to look back at the shape of it.

The moment of use is two seconds long and often one-handed: after the biscuit,
after the walk, after the third episode. Anything that asks a question at that
moment is a reason not to open the app tomorrow.

The job to be done: *"I want to know, without keeping a diary, whether I am
mostly doing well."*

## Product purpose

Two buttons. Green was a good choice, red was a bad one. Tapping records the
choice and the moment, and that is the entire input surface of the product.

The output is the recap: pick a stretch of time and watch every choice you made
in it rain down and settle into a grid. Most of it green and you can see that in
half a second, without reading a number.

Success looks like a tap that costs nothing, and a recap that tells the truth
faster than a sentence could.

## Brand personality

**Blunt, wordless, unbothered.**

There are two colours and almost no text. The app does not praise a green tap or
tut at a red one — a streak counter would turn an honest record into a game you
can lose, and the moment it is a game people stop tapping red.

Emotionally the target is *recognition*: the grid shows you something you already
half knew.

## Anti-references

- **Habit trackers with streaks.** Streaks punish honesty. There is no streak
  here, no badge, no "you're on fire", no notification asking where you were.
- **Mood journals.** No note field, no tags, no "how are you feeling?". The
  whole point is that there is nothing to fill in.
- **Dashboards.** No line chart, no weekly average, no percentage-better-than-
  last-month. The grid is the report.
- **Anything that needs an account to start.** The first tap happens before
  anyone has been asked who they are.

## Design principles

1. **The two buttons are the app.** They fill the screen. Everything else is one
   small button in a corner, and that button is also where the account lives,
   because settings are not a destination.
2. **A tap must land instantly.** It is written locally first and always
   succeeds. The network is a backup, never a gate.
3. **Nothing before the first tap.** No sign-up, no tour, no permission prompt.
   An account is offered only when somebody goes looking for one.
4. **Colour carries the meaning, but never alone.** Green and red are the whole
   visual language, so every place they decide something also says it in a word
   or a shape — red-green colour blindness is common and this app is two
   colours.
5. **The recap is a spectacle, once.** The squares falling is the one piece of
   theatre in the product, it lasts a few seconds, and it can be skipped by
   tapping. It ends on something you can read at a glance and sit with.
